# Session guide — about 45 minutes

Ask in whatever language the participant is most comfortable in. Write their answers down in their
words, in that language. Translate later, for the write-up only.

## 0 · Before you start (2 min)

Consent, per [`consent.md`](consent.md). Say plainly: *"I am looking for the places this is wrong or
not worth the effort. That is the useful answer, not the polite one."*

## 1 · Their world, before Ghostlight exists (12 min)

**Do not mention the tool yet.** Nothing in this section should hint at what it does.

1. Walk me through one prop sequence from a recent show — who carries what, from where, in which cue.
2. When was the last time a prop was in the wrong place or with the wrong person before a cue? What
   happened?
3. What did your run sheet *not* say that would have caught it?
4. How do you find and fix that kind of problem today? Which tool, and is it good enough?
5. Do you actually distinguish two identical props, consumables, and who hands over to whom? If not,
   why not — is it not worth it, or is there nowhere to write it?
6. In a normal line of your paperwork, is the source and holder already written, or would you have
   to add it?

**Record before moving on:** how long they spend reading, preparing and correcting a sheet today;
how many fields a line has; how often they have to go and ask someone; how they trace back to the
cue that caused a problem; whether the printed version matters.

## 2 · Paper test (8 min)

Give them the **Missing Handoff** scenario on paper, as a short run sheet. Do not say there is a
fault in it.

- Can they see the problem? How long does it take?
- How do they describe it in their own words?

This is the control. If they find it instantly on paper without the tool, that is a finding, and it
belongs in the write-up.

## 3 · The tool (15 min)

Now introduce it. **Two separate timings — note both:**

- **Instruction time**: how long until they can use it without help.
- **Task time**: how long the actual work takes afterwards.

Ask them to:

1. Open the same scenario and say what the screen is telling them.
2. Find the cause of the problem without being told where to look.
3. Fix it, and say whether the result is what they expected.
4. Type in a short sequence of their own — their real one if they have it, otherwise one they
   invent while you watch.

**Watch for and write down:** where they hesitate, what they expect to be clickable and is not, what
vocabulary they use that the app does not, and the exact point where typing starts to feel like too
much work. That last one is the whole hypothesis.

## 4 · The questions that decide it (6 min)

1. Which part of the typing was worth it? Which part was more annoying than paper or your
   spreadsheet?
2. Is there a specific real job this season where you would use the output? Which one?
3. What would stop you using it?
4. If a colleague asked whether this is worth the bother, what would you tell them?
5. Anything that would have to be true before you would type a whole show into it?

**"I could see myself using it" is not a yes.** Push once for the specific job and the specific
blocker. If the answer stays general, record it as general — that is the finding.

## 5 · Close (2 min)

Thank them. Confirm what you may quote and whether they want to see it first. Ask if they would look
at it again later, and whether there is anyone else worth speaking to.

---

## Straight after, before you do anything else

Write the findings up within the hour, while you still remember the hesitations. Use
[`findings-template.md`](findings-template.md). Start with whatever contradicts the idea.
